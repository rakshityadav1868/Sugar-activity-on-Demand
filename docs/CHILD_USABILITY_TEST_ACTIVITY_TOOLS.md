# Child usability check: Activity Tools

This is a short observation protocol for the optional right-side Activity
Tools drawer. It is a test plan, not evidence that a child test has already
been conducted.

## Question

Can a child discover the drawer, understand its two choices, complete the
reflection quest, save a thought, and turn that thought into a safe activity
change without adult instruction?

## Participants and safety

- Test with 5–8 children from the intended age range, one at a time.
- Obtain the consent and supervision required by the school or guardian.
- Do not collect names, accounts, faces, audio, or unrelated Journal content.
- Record interaction observations and anonymous counts only.
- Tell the child that the product is being tested—not the child—and that they
  can stop at any time.

## Setup

Use a generated activity with an obvious playable goal and one imperfect but
non-blocking detail. Start in Preview with Activity Tools closed. Use the same
activity and screen size for every participant.

## Script

Avoid pointing at controls or using their labels before the child finds them.

1. “Please try this activity.” Let the child play for 2–3 minutes.
2. “Find a way to tell the Studio what you noticed.”
3. If the child opens Activity Tools, ask: “What do you think these two choices
   do?” Do not correct the answer yet.
4. “Find a way to explain what happened while you played.”
5. Let the child use the Try → Notice → Explain → Imagine quest and save one
   reflection.
6. “Can you use your idea to ask for a change, but stop before applying it?”
7. End with: “What was fun, confusing, or unnecessary?” and “What would you
   call this button?”

If the child is stuck for 20 seconds, use this hint ladder and record the
highest level needed:

1. “Look near the activity controls.”
2. “Look for something that could help you change the activity.”
3. Point to the **Change** button without clicking it.

## What to record

For each participant, record:

- whether **Change** was found without a hint;
- time to first open;
- whether “Change this activity” and “Understand & reflect” were explained in
  the child’s own words;
- highest hint level needed;
- whether the four-step quest was completed without adult navigation;
- whether a meaningful reflection was saved (do not judge spelling);
- whether “Turn into a change” was understood before clicking;
- any accidental application, lost work, layout problem, or blocked preview;
- the child’s suggested label and closing comment.

## Success criteria for the next design decision

- At least 4 of 5 children find **Change** with no more than hint level 1.
- At least 4 of 5 distinguish changing the artifact from reflecting on it.
- At least 4 of 5 complete the quest and save a thought without navigation
  help.
- No child loses the accepted activity version or applies a change without the
  review step.
- The drawer never opens automatically and never makes the activity unusable.

If discoverability fails but the quest succeeds after opening, test the entry
label separately (for example **Change**, **Remix**, or **Activity Tools**)
instead of redesigning the reflection content. If comprehension fails inside
the drawer, revise one label or step at a time and repeat the same protocol.
